Case study · 2025 — 2026

DeepDrishti

Drishti means vision. We set out to build a platform that helps people see whether a face in an image or video is real — and, just as importantly, why the model thinks so.

Role
Team Lead · ML & Dashboard UX

01 — The problem

Face-swaps are getting harder to spot — and a bare “fake / real” score isn't enough.

Manipulated media spreads faster than it can be checked. Most detection tools return a single number with no context, which makes it hard for a reviewer to trust the result, explain it to someone else, or act on it.

Our goal was a system that detects face-swaps accurately and communicates its reasoning: where in the frame, when in the video, and how confident.

02 — How it works

From upload to evidence.

  1. 01UploadImage or video
  2. 02Face extractionDetect & align faces per frame
  3. 03Deep modelFace-swap classification
  4. 04Temporal checksAnomalous frames, audio–video sync
  5. 05Explain & reportHeatmaps + forensic PDF

03 — What we built

Deep learning detection

Trained models that reach 93% accuracy on face-swap detection datasets.

Anomaly frame detection

Pinpoints the exact segments of a video that look manipulated, instead of judging the clip as a whole.

Audio–video consistency

Temporal feature visualisation that flags when lip movement and audio drift apart.

Explainable heatmaps

XAI overlays show which facial regions drove the model's decision, so reviewers can verify it.

Interactive dashboard

Confidence scores and detection results visualised so a non-expert can read them at a glance.

Automated reports

One-click authenticity reports for forensic analysis and record-keeping.

04 — Design decisions

Trust comes from showing the work.

As team lead I split my time between the model pipeline and the interface. The key UX decision was to never show a score alone: every verdict sits next to the evidence behind it — the heatmap, the flagged frames, the sync graph.

We used a calm, neutral palette and reserved warm, alarming colours only for manipulated regions, so the eye goes straight to what matters.
